§ 01 — The problem

A small dataset invites overfitting.

Binary posture classification sounds trivial until you count the images. A modest labelled set of sitting and standing photos gives a CNN more than enough room to memorise the training examples — nailing them while learning nothing that transfers to a new photo.

So the real task wasn't "build a CNN"; it was "build a CNN that generalises from limited data," which turns the project into a study of regularisation and augmentation rather than architecture.

A model that aces training and flunks test hasn't learned — it's memorised.

§ 02 — Constraints

Guardrails against memorising.

  • Augment aggressively.

    Rotation, width/height shifts, and horizontal flips synthesise variety the small dataset lacks, so the model sees a new-ish image every epoch.

  • Regularise the classifier head.

    Dropout between dense layers keeps the network from leaning on any single feature to fit the training set.

  • Evaluate honestly.

    A held-out test split plus training/validation accuracy and loss curves, so overfitting shows up as a diverging gap rather than hiding.

§ 03 — Architecture

Load, augment, learn, judge.

Images and CSV labels are loaded and resized to 224×224, encoded to categorical, split train/test, then fed through three conv blocks into a dense classifier — trained on an augmenting data generator.

§ 04 — What the curves showed

Augmentation bought the generalisation.

The training and validation curves are the whole story. Without augmentation the two lines split early — training accuracy climbing while validation stalls, the textbook shape of a network memorising its inputs. With augmentation the gap narrows, and the test accuracy becomes a number you can actually believe.

Plotting accuracy and loss side by side made that trade-off visible rather than assumed — which is exactly why the evaluation plots are part of the deliverable, not an afterthought.

§ 05 — Keeping it honest

The plots are the point.

It would have been easy to report a single test-accuracy figure and stop. Shipping the accuracy and loss curves instead admits where the model is weak and shows the augmentation earning its place — the difference between a result you can defend and a number you're hoping nobody probes.

§ 06 — What I'd do differently

Three things, next time.

  • Start from a pretrained backbone.

    Transfer learning from a network like MobileNet would beat a from-scratch CNN on a dataset this small, with far less overfitting risk.

  • Report a confusion matrix.

    Knowing whether errors skew toward false-sitting or false-standing tells you more than a single accuracy figure.

  • Use a proper validation split.

    Validating on the test set inflates confidence; a distinct validation set would make early-stopping and tuning honest.
